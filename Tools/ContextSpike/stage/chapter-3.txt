Chapter Three - The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter

The gulls had gone quiet by the time Maren reached the headland. Below her the sea moved like something breathing in its sleep, and the lamp at the top of the tower turned its slow, patient eye across the water.
Her father had kept this light for thirty-one years. He had never once let it go dark, not in the storm of '74, not the night her mother left. Now the Trust wanted to automate it, and the letter sat folded in her coat pocket like a stone.
She climbed the hundred and twelve steps without counting them. At the top, the old logbook lay open to a page dated in his careful hand: "Visibility poor. Wind SW 7. All well."
