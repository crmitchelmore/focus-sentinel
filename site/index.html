How a day goes

Four moments, in order.

There is no dashboard to tend. You write a sentence, start a session, answer the occasional question, and read the record when you want to.

  1. Write the priority as one sentence.

    Focus keeps one priority. Setting a new one replaces it and moves the old one to Recent. If the sentence has no verb or no outcome, Focus suggests a rewrite; you can keep your own wording. Add a Done when line so you know what finished looks like.

    Priority Draft the grant budget  ·  Done when Every line has a number

  2. Start a session, with a timebox if you want one.

    Pause it, take a break, log a detour when you leave the work on purpose, and finish it when you stop. Stopping early asks why; every field on that form is optional. The menu bar shows the timer.

  3. Get a question, not a verdict.

    Focus compares the app in front with the priority's context pack. When you drift for a while, the mark in the menu bar changes and nothing else happens. If the drift continues, a small panel names the app and offers a way back. When Focus cannot tell, it asks whether the app is part of the work. Your answer becomes a rule, and it stops asking.

    A small question.

    Is Numbers part of Draft the grant budget?

    Focus has not been told which apps this task lives in. One answer covers this whole app, for this priority.

    Yes, add itNo, it isn'tNot now

  4. Read the record.

    History lists what Focus did today and why: each nudge, where it was shown, what you answered. If it should have spoken up and did not, you can say so there. Records older than the retention limit you choose are deleted once a day.

What it does

Small instruments, each with one job.

  • The context pack

    Apps, sites, keywords and files that belong to the priority. Add them on the Details tab, or drop files and links onto the window. Recent priorities come back with their pack and rules.

  • Rules you can read

    Mark an app or a site as related or unrelated, for this priority or for this session only. A rule settles that case for good. The rules are deterministic, so the same facts always get the same answer.

  • Timeboxes that never stop you

    Give the priority a limit and Focus shows the time left, then the time over. Sessions keep the wall clock: a Mac that slept for an hour has spent that hour, and the app says so.

  • A menu-bar mark

    The mark shows what Focus currently believes. Click it for the priority, the timer, any open question and the session controls. A global shortcut sets a new priority from inside any app.

  • Quiet when it should be

    Focus caps how often it interrupts each day and stays quiet while you present, while a microphone is in use, while you are away from the keyboard, and during the quiet hours you set.

  • Interrupt me less

    One switch sends nudges and questions to the menu bar instead of opening panels. Reduce Motion turns it on for you. Every panel can be answered, snoozed or closed.

  • A tour on the real window

    The walkthrough takes you to each control in turn, highlights it, and offers to do the step for you. Replay it any time from Help.

  • Yours to export or delete

    Export your activity data or your settings as separate files. Delete everything from Privacy settings, and Focus restarts with an empty data file.

Privacy

Your data stays on this Mac.

Focus has no account or sync and sends no usage reports. What follows is the app's own wording from its Privacy settings, and the build gate checks the shipped binary against it.

  • What it reads

    Which app is active. If a browser shares its site without a permission request, Focus reads the host name only.

  • What it never reads

    Window titles, full web addresses, file paths, document names, or anything you type.

  • No network

    Focus never connects to the internet unless you turn on update checks. When it checks, it sends its name and version and nothing about you.

  • Permissions are yours to give

    Focus asks macOS for nothing unless you switch it on in Privacy settings. Accessibility and Screen Recording are listed there, off, with a plain note of what each would let it read.

  • Away means quiet

    No keyboard or mouse for a minute, or as long as you choose, and Focus stops counting time and stays quiet until you are back.

  • You can check

    The source is public under the MIT licence. The data file is one folder in Application Support, and Privacy settings show it in the Finder.

The companion is decoration, not an indicator of surveillance. Nothing in the app praises, scores or scolds; a nudge describes the activity, and a question is a real question.

Who it is for

People whose work lives across many apps.

  • Writers and researchers who keep one question open for a day and drift into a browser without noticing.
  • Engineers and designers whose task spans an editor, a terminal, a few sites and a handful of files, and who want the app to know that list rather than guess.
  • Anyone on calls who needs an app that stays quiet while a microphone is live, while presenting, and after hours.
  • People who read the privacy page first. Every signal is listed, every permission is off until you switch it on, and the record can be inspected, exported or deleted.

Questions

Straight answers.

Does it read my screen?

No. Focus does not request Screen Recording or Accessibility permission in this version, and nothing in this version reads what those would unlock. It reads which app is active and, where a browser shares it freely, the site's host name only.

Does it phone home?

Focus never connects to the internet unless you turn on update checks in General settings. Those are off until you turn them on, and a check carries the app's name and version and nothing about you.

Does it block apps or sites?

No. Focus does not block, hide or close anything, and a timebox never stops you working. It notices, it asks, and it offers a way back.

What happens on a call or a presentation?

A running microphone keeps Focus quiet, because a call is not a distraction. It also stays quiet while you present, while you are away, and during quiet hours. You can switch the microphone rule off, and then a nudge may arrive mid-call.

Where is my data, and can I take it with me?

In one folder under Application Support on your Mac; Privacy settings show it in the Finder. Export your activity data and your settings as separate files from the File menu. Delete everything from Privacy settings; that cannot be undone.

When can I download it?

There is no public build yet. The release tracker on GitHub lists what remains before the first signed, notarised build: crmitchelmore/focus-sentinel, issue 12. Updates, once shipped, arrive only if you switch update checks on.
