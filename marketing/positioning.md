# Positioning

Every claim here is checked in `claims-audit.md`. If a feature is not in that file, do not say it.

## One line

**Focus Sentinel keeps one priority in view on your Mac and asks, rather than accuses, when the
app in front seems to have drifted from it.**

## 25 words

A local-only Mac companion for one written priority. It notices which app is in front, asks when
unsure, never blocks anything, keeps no score, and never goes online.

## 50 words

Focus Sentinel is a calm companion for the Mac. Write today's priority as one sentence. Focus
compares the app in front with it, asks a plain question when it cannot tell, and turns your
answer into a rule. It blocks nothing, keeps no score, asks for no permissions and never
connects to the internet.

## Who it is for, and what hurts

| audience | the pain | what Focus does about it |
|---|---|---|
| **Writers and researchers** | One question stays open all day, and a browser tab takes twenty minutes before anyone notices. | Keeps the sentence in view. A drift first changes the menu-bar mark, and a panel follows only if it lasts. |
| **Engineers and designers** | The work spans an editor, a terminal, a few sites and some files. A blocker cannot tell work from distraction in the same app. | You name the context. Focus asks about anything it cannot place, once per app per priority, and remembers the answer as a rule you can read. |
| **People on calls** | Pop-ups during a call or a shared screen are embarrassing. | Quiet while a microphone is live, while an app is full screen, while you are away, and in quiet hours you set. |
| **The privacy-first** | Most focus tools want an account, cloud sync or Screen Recording. | No account and no network. It asks for no permission unless you switch the ask on. It reads the app's name only, stays out of password managers and banking apps, and lets you export or delete everything. |
| **People burnt by gamified tools** | Streaks and scores turn a bad afternoon into a verdict. | No score, no streak, no daily total. History is a plain ledger of what Focus did and why. |

## How it differs: categories, not competitors

We compare categories, not named products. We have not verified competitors' feature lists, so
we make no claims about them.

| category | what it usually does | how Focus Sentinel differs |
|---|---|---|
| **App and site blockers** | Stop you opening things on a list or a schedule. | Blocks nothing. It notices, asks, and offers a way back. It handles apps that are both work and distraction by asking instead of guessing. |
| **Screen-time limiters** | Daily limits, often with streaks or a score. | No limits on you and no score. The daily limit is on how often *Focus* may interrupt *you*. |
| **Session timers** | Count time and do not know what you are doing. | Timeboxes tied to one written priority. They show time left, then time over, and never stop you working. |
| **Automatic time trackers** | Record activity in detail and categorise it for reports, often syncing it. | Records only which app was in front and what Focus itself did. Kept on this Mac, deleted on a schedule you choose. No dashboard. |

**The one thing only we say:** *it asks instead of accusing.* Focus will not call something a
distraction unless you have told it what your work looks like. If it is unsure, it asks.

## Objections

| objection | answer |
|---|---|
| "This is surveillance." | It reads which app is active and nothing else. No window titles, web addresses, file names, screenshots or keystrokes. It never connects to the internet. Privacy settings list every signal it can see. |
| "I need a blocker, not a nudge." | Then use a blocker. Focus is for work that lives in the same apps as the distractions, where blocking breaks the work. |
| "It'll nag me." | It has a daily limit (eight interruptions by default), quiet hours, silence on calls and in full screen, and an "Interrupt me less" switch that keeps everything in the menu bar. Snooze or dismiss any panel. |
| "Can it tell YouTube from documentation?" | Not in this version. Focus judges apps, not tabs. It does not read which site a browser shows. For a browser it asks you, and it remembers your answer for that priority. |
| "What if it gets it wrong?" | Mark it relevant in one click, or tell History it should have spoken. Either way it writes a rule you can read and delete. Privacy settings show the reasons behind its current assessment. |
| "Does it use AI?" | No model judges you in this version. The engine is deterministic: the same facts always give the same answer. |
| "Do I need an account or subscription?" | No account. Price is still to be decided. |
| "Intel Mac? Older macOS?" | macOS 26 or later. Tested on Apple silicon. |
| "Is it open source?" | Planned under the MIT licence. **Only say "open source" once the repository is public and has a LICENSE file.** |
| "Does it work with macOS Focus modes?" | Separately. Focus Sentinel cannot read macOS Focus modes. Set its quiet hours in its own settings. |

## Messaging pillars

1. **One priority, kept in view.** Write it as one sentence and add "Done when". The menu bar
   shows it with the timer.
2. **It asks instead of accusing.** Uncertainty becomes a question, and your answer becomes a
   rule you can read. It nudges only when you have told it what the work looks like.
3. **Quiet when it should be.** Daily limit, calls, full screen, away, quiet hours, and Interrupt
   me less.
4. **Your data stays on this Mac.** No network, no account, no permissions by default. Private
   apps are left alone. Export or delete everything.
5. **Nothing is scored.** No streaks or tallies. History records what the app did, not how you
   did.

("10x"), or claims we cannot show in the app.
